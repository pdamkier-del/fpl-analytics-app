## Confirmed priorities

1. **Unify the validated forecast used by player views and private planning.** `scripts/integrate_desktop_probabilities.py` deliberately routes read-only views to the GK forecast while private clients retain their locked forecast/identity contract. In the planning snapshot (20 October 14:23 UTC), Penders has xM 78.00, PM E[min] 77.44, xP 3.905. The public GK snapshot (14:33 UTC) has xM 89.47 and xP 3.800. This is a model-variant integration gap, not proof that increasing minutes always increases simulated points. A URL swap is insufficient: validate the bridge, worker identity, and full six-GW chain before changing B planning.

2. **Use penalty hierarchy as evidence for taker allocation.** `scripts/run_live_vfinal_penalty_state.py` normalizes observed penalty attempts plus a small goal-rate prior; the official taker order is not used. In the matching archived catalogue, Palmer and Saka are both first-choice takers, yet their model shares are respectively 4.29% and 90.08%. These are allocation weights conditional on a team penalty, not probabilities of receiving a penalty. This known issue remains unresolved. Combine role, availability, history and uncertainty; validate role changes and players without recent attempts. Keep penalty occurrence, taker allocation and conversion separate.

3. **Align the MM minute expectation with PM's actual distribution.** `joint_simulator.py` draws rounded, bounded Normal durations. Its location parameter is not its resulting expectation: location 90 and SD 10 produces E[min] 86.01 after clipping. Across 3,570 outfield fixture rows, mean absolute MM–PM discrepancy is 0.851 minutes; 915 rows exceed one minute. Saka is 84.56 versus 80.89; Palmer 84.93 versus 81.33. Repair the duration distribution and contract together. True E[min]=90 on [0,90] cannot coexist with a nonzero early-exit probability. Existing opt-in mean-preserving and survival models should be assessed rather than duplicated; their existence is not evidence of deployment readiness.

4. **Value legal automatic substitutions in the TS objective.** `transfer_planner.projected_manager_score` uses `optimize.plan_squad`, whose score credits XI and captain but omits expected bench replacement points. A controlled legal-position synthetic squad, independent keepers, and certain outfield starters reproduces this: raising a guaranteed-playing reserve keeper from 2 to 3 xP leaves score unchanged at 70, despite a 20% starter absence probability and a true expected gain of 0.2 points. This is not the user's team. Implement legal autosubs through shared scenarios, rather than adding every bench player's points. Captain fallback also currently uses marginal probabilities; dependence matters for fallback and substitution decisions, though it is not needed to sum a fixed XI's unconditional expected points.

5. **Complete event consistency and FPL assist attribution.** The locked simulator samples red-card and own-goal debits after goals/minutes/clean sheets. It lacks a red-card departure timestamp and its effect on subsequent scoring; an own-goal debit does not itself become an opponent scoring event. Generic assist allocation does not explicitly represent all FPL penalty-winning, rebound and forced-own-goal cases. Public GK forecasts already enable joint goalkeeper and starter sampling; it would be incorrect to describe joint lineups as absent everywhere. Existing opt-in correctness work should be reused. The corrected-input historical audit found combined xP-MAE improvements of 0.003645 (2024) and 0.005838 (2025), but some minute/RMSE metrics worsened and the data were reused for development. That supports further validation, not automatic promotion. See `2026-10-13-simulator-correctness.md`.

## Research candidates, not demonstrated upgrades

- Assess close transfer/captain decisions across simulation seeds. Four hundred draws per fixture can make small differences unstable. Outcome quantiles are not confidence intervals for estimated xP or parameter uncertainty. Increase draws adaptively where decisions change, and measure decision stability.
- Test future injury-return and hierarchy scenarios, score-dependent match dynamics, and opponent-lineup effects. MM already includes rest, congestion, role jumps and return information; these are refinements, not entirely missing factors.
- Assess transfer horizon and future chip option value. Six-GW forecasts cannot certify a whole half-season chip optimum; current search is heuristic with fixed discount/hit buffers. Compare policy performance in historical rollouts before claiming better decisions.

## Validation limits and order

The new diagnostic completed successfully, including the actual-optimizer bench assertion. This review did not run a new full historical model comparison or regression suite. Existing historical six-GW coverage is incomplete (33/183 cells), and historical publication times often use kickoff-offset proxies. New acceptance work must respect temporal cutoffs, use paired seeds/holdouts, and assess start Brier, minute MAE/calibration, xP MAE/bias, and final TS outcomes separately.

Recommended order: forecast identity/integration and penalty roles; minute contract; autosub/captain scenarios; event consistency; then research candidates. Previous general finishing, shot-level skill and difficulty-weighting experiments did not establish a robust improvement worth promoting. Finishing can matter physically without our current estimator improving out-of-sample forecasts. Production and the published app remain unchanged.
